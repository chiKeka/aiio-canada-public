## Verified local electrical context

[Job Bank's Calgary-region electrician profile](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/marketreport/outlook-occupation/20684/geo25411), receipt `capacity-calgary-electrician-jobbank/receipt.json`, identifies NOC 72200, electricians excluding industrial and power-system electricians. It reports approximately 6,290 workers, with 88% in construction. The profile labels the 2023–2025 recent assessment a labour shortage, updated July 28, 2026. Its separate December 10, 2025 forecast assesses 2025–2027 employment prospects as Moderate. The page modification date is August 7, 2026. Those periods and dates must remain separate; the workforce fact does not state its own base year on this page.

Permitted use: corroborate the choice of a Calgary-area installation-electrician market, retain historical tightness and forward outlook as distinct contextual evidence, and flag occupation compatibility. Neither the headcount nor the construction share reveals who is uncommitted to other work, available when, sufficiently qualified, or willing to commute to either site. Do not multiply 6,290 by a paid-hour calendar and call the result deployable capacity. Do not map this occupation to power-system line work or industrial electricians without reconciling their different scope.

[Job Bank's outlook methodology](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/trend-analysis/search-job-outlooks/outlooks-methodology), receipt `capacity-jobbank-outlook-method/receipt.json`, explains the employment-growth, replacement, experienced-unemployed and composite indicators behind the ratings. Its occupational projections use industry forecasts, Labour Force Survey/Census information and regional analysis; experienced unemployment uses Employment Insurance information. The categorical outlook is a job-market forecast, not an employer crew-availability survey. [The recent-trends methodology](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/trend-analysis/job-market-reports/recent-trends-methodology), receipt `capacity-jobbank-trends-method/receipt.json`, is a separate historical market assessment. A regional shortage classification supplies neither a project-specific vacancy count nor monthly unserved hours.

## Province and catchment context

[BuildForce Canada's July 20, 2026 Alberta release](https://www.buildforce.ca/en/press-release/construction-activity-rises-in-alberta-to-2035-as-non-residential-growth-offsets-moderating-residential-demands/), receipt `capacity-alberta-buildforce/receipt.json`, reports 196,700 construction tradespeople in 2025 and projects non-residential employment 15% higher by 2035. It describes a possible cumulative recruitment gap of 5,300 by 2035. This is provincial stock and a long-term forecast, not Calgary installation electricians available in 2026–2027. The forecast includes retirement and entry assumptions; do not deduct retirement again from an already net trajectory. It supports private/industrial/utility maintenance demand as relevant background, not a numerical private package allocation.

[Job Bank's Alberta construction profile](https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/trend-analysis/job-market-reports/alberta/sectoral-profile-construction), receipt `capacity-alberta-construction-profile/receipt.json`, reports 246,500 construction-industry workers provincially in 2024, including 94,600 in the Calgary economic region. This is all construction employment, not the construction-trade universe used by BuildForce or the NOC-specific Job Bank count. Its dated 2025 profile also describes mobility; a general mobility statement does not identify an eligible electrical crew pool or incremental monthly supply for these two projects. Preserve the populations and vintages separately.

[Statistics Canada table 14-10-0466-03](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1410046603), receipt `capacity-statcan-regional-occupation/receipt.json`, is annual employment by occupation and economic region, derived from the Labour Force Survey; the landing page reports a January 9, 2026 release. The retained landing page establishes the table's scope but does not supply an extracted Calgary trade observation. No numerical value was recovered or promoted from the unpopulated interactive table. Even a properly selected employment series would be workforce stock, not contractor backlog or uncommitted paid-hour capacity.

## Evidence required before changing pilot capacity

The supported-use distinction is now explicit: Job Bank/BuildForce/Statistics Canada provide stock, outlook and historical market context; actual model capacity is still null. To estimate deployable paid-hour capacity, a dated contractor or trade-dispatch record would need the specific installation trade and certification, relevant travel catchment, qualified headcount, paid-hour calendar, already committed backlog, available mobilization dates and any separately additional mobility. It must reconcile electricians, industrial electricians and line/power-system workers and preserve apprentice/supervision restrictions.

For the named intersection, dated school/CAL3 package awards or prime/subcontract schedules need to establish the installer identities, actual overlapping installation windows, quantities/paid hours and remaining commitments. No such public record was verified in this scoped follow-up. This is an acquisition gap, not evidence of no contractors or no competition. Keep the normalized reverse-stress thresholds conditional and preserve ample-capacity and protected-public-work no-impact controls.
