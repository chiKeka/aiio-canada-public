# Research charter

## Program name

**AI Infrastructure Impact Observatory Canada (AIIO Canada)**

**Tagline:** Tracking how AI investment changes the cost, capacity, and delivery of Canada’s public infrastructure.

## Purpose

AIIO Canada is a dual-use academic and public-interest research program. It will produce defensible research findings while giving policymakers, infrastructure owners, researchers and the public a legible way to inspect the evidence, assumptions and scenarios behind those findings.

The Observatory studies competition for real resources. It does not ask whether AI is desirable in the abstract. It asks how data-centre construction and operation interact with labour, materials, electrical systems, land, permitting and project schedules—and how those interactions change the public sector’s ability to deliver infrastructure.

## Primary question

How, when, and where does AI infrastructure capital expenditure reshape the cost, capacity, and delivery of public infrastructure across Canada?

## Alberta pilot question

If $50 billion of AI infrastructure investment occurs in Alberta between 2027 and 2036, how could its construction and operating demands propagate through skilled trades, major materials, electricity generation and transmission, and the schedules and costs of public projects?

## Scope

### Geography

- Pilot: Alberta.
- Expansion: all provinces and territories using a common Canada-ready schema.
- Outputs must retain geographic resolution and cannot silently substitute national averages for provincial conditions.

### Infrastructure

- Core public portfolio: hospitals, schools and government facilities.
- Extended public portfolio: roads, rail, municipal buildings, water and wastewater.
- Enabling systems: regulated electricity and gas utilities and other material network constraints.

### Evidence

- Publicly accessible sources only.
- Preferred sources: Statistics Canada, provincial and municipal open-data portals, system operators, regulators, government budget/capital-plan documents, public procurement notices, company filings and official announcements.
- Media and analyst reports may aid discovery but cannot be the sole support for a material model input.

## Intended users

- Infrastructure ministries and agencies planning capital programs.
- Utilities, municipalities and regulators assessing coincident capacity needs.
- Researchers examining capital-project systems and regional resource constraints.
- Journalists and members of the public who need transparent, non-technical explanations.

## Research outputs

- Living source and evidence register.
- Weekly evidence digest.
- Versioned Alberta baseline and scenario datasets.
- Graph-based propagation model with sensitivity and validation reports.
- Interactive public website and downloadable evidence.
- Auditable Excel workbook that reproduces selected outputs without becoming the system of record.
- Methods papers and a Canada expansion protocol.

## Success criteria for the Alberta pilot

1. Each material input is traceable to a public source or an explicit assumption.
2. The model identifies pressure mechanisms, not only correlations.
3. Scenario results carry ranges and sensitivities, not false precision.
4. A user can explain why an output changed by following the graph path and input lineage.
5. Website, dataset and workbook outputs reconcile to the same versioned model run.
6. Independent review finds no unlabelled scenario values or unsupported causal claims.

## Boundaries

- The Observatory does not predict individual project outcomes or make investment recommendations.
- Announced capex is not treated as committed, spent or locally captured without evidence.
- Electrical energy, peak demand, connection requests and transmission capacity are distinct quantities.
- Model pressure scores are diagnostic indices unless and until econometric calibration supports physical-unit claims.
- Market-wide reference-index projections and AI-attributable increments are separate estimands; neither may be inferred from the other.
- The Observatory is a research instrument. Academic use requires source verification, institutional ethics review where applicable, and disclosure of material AI assistance.
