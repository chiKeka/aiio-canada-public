# Evidence and publication policy

## Core rule

The Observatory publishes the evidence chain, not only a result. Readers must be able to distinguish what a source reported, what the research team calculated, what was assumed and what exists only inside a scenario.

## Claim classes

- **Observation:** a source-reported value or statement.
- **Corroborated fact:** an observation supported independently.
- **Inference:** a reproducible calculation or classification based on observations.
- **Assumption:** a selected parameter whose uncertainty is tested.
- **Scenario result:** a conditional output from declared inputs.

The website, workbook, digest and downloadable data use the same labels and definitions.

## Source requirements

- Link to the most authoritative available public source.
- Record publisher, title, date, retrieval time and a stable locator.
- Preserve the raw file or record its content hash when licence/access conditions prevent redistribution.
- Record revisions and corrections; do not silently overwrite a prior published release.
- Use media sources primarily for discovery or to document public claims. Material inputs require an official or primary source where possible.

## Review

- Material manually extracted values require a second-person check before release.
- Causal edges require a stated mechanism and evidence grade.
- Scenario assumptions require a rationale, range and sensitivity result.
- Weekly digests distinguish new evidence from changes to the model.
- Independent review comments and their dispositions are retained with each major release.

## Corrections

Corrections identify the affected release, original value or claim, corrected content, reason, date and downstream outputs that changed. Corrected releases remain reproducible.

## Public language

- Do not call scenarios forecasts.
- Do not equate announced, committed, under-construction and spent capital.
- Do not express an index as dollars, workers, MW or delay without a calibrated conversion.
- Do not express a pressure score as a percentage; for example, 0.24 is not a 24% delay.
- Describe v0.2 low/central/high outputs as structural cases, not probability or uncertainty intervals.
- Keep the independent MW power overlay visually and structurally separate from the CAD pressure scenario.
- State uncertainty in the same visual field as the result.
- Avoid project-level predictions when only system-level pressure is supported.
